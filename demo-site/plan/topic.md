# Demo site topic — Concrete Utopias

The Contact Sheet demo site is a photo blog about **Soviet-era brutalist and
modernist architecture** — the monumental concrete ambition of the USSR and its
sphere (roughly 1955–1991), photographed across two intertwined threads:

1. **Still in use** — the palaces, ministries, institutes, circuses,
   sanatoriums and housing blocks that are *still working buildings* in
   countries that kept evolving. Adapted, re-purposed, patched with satellite
   dishes and new glazing, but alive — Soviet form carrying post-Soviet life.
2. **Abandoned, still worth the trip** — the derelict but spectacular relics:
   empty sanatoriums, unfinished auditoriums, a flying-saucer party
   headquarters on a mountain, a radar the size of a district. Buildings whose
   visual impact and ambitious history outlive their function.

The subject suits the theme's stripped-down, film-inspired design: monumental
concrete geometry, faded Soviet color palettes (ochre, teal, oxblood, mosaic
gold), and dramatic form all read strongly in the photo-strip and archive grids.

## Sub-topics

Each seeds a set of demo posts and spans the *in-use ↔ abandoned* range.

1. **Spomenik** — the abstract WWII memorial monuments of the former
   Yugoslavia: futurist concrete sculpture marooned in remote landscapes, some
   tended, some forgotten.
2. **Palaces of the People** — still-working civic monuments: palaces of
   culture, circuses, wedding palaces, sports halls — bold sculptural concrete
   still hosting everyday public life.
3. **Ministries & Institutes** — governmental and scientific megastructures
   still in daily use: stacked-beam towers, research institutes, the boldest
   bureaucratic statements in concrete.
4. **Sanatorium Dreams** — the utopian health-resort architecture of the Black
   Sea and Caucasus: some still taking guests, some slowly reclaimed by ferns.
5. **Abandoned Futures** — derelict structures still worth visiting for their
   sheer visual power: a saucer on a summit, a ghost auditorium, a lattice
   radar at the edge of an exclusion zone.
6. **Microrayon** — the residential megablocks (panelki) still lived in across
   evolving cities: everyday Soviet modernism, adapted with glazed balconies,
   dishes and fresh paint.
7. **Cosmic Ambition** — the space-age technological monuments: flying-saucer
   institutes, cosmonaut memorials, structures built to look like the future
   itself.

## Why this topic

- Monumental concrete geometry and dramatic sculptural form photograph
  strongly in both high-contrast monochrome and faded Soviet color.
- The *still-in-use vs abandoned* duality gives the demo genuine range —
  peopled, living buildings beside silent, overgrown ruins.
- Rich real-world references (Tbilisi, Buzludzha, Tskaltubo, Pripyat, Podgarić,
  Yerevan, Kyiv…) ground the images in an ambitious, specific history.
- A deliberate mix of color and monochrome across the posts keeps the site
  varied without breaking the overall mood.
